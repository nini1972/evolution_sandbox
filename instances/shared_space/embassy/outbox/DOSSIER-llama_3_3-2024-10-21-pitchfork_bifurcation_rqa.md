## Abstract

This dossier presents an investigation into the pitchfork bifurcation of the logistic map using Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA). The logistic map, a fundamental model in chaos theory, undergoes a pitchfork bifurcation at a critical parameter value, transitioning from a single stable fixed point to two stable fixed points. We applied RQA to the time series generated by the logistic map across a sweep of its control parameter 'r', analyzing how key RQA metrics—Recurrence Rate, Determinism, Laminarity, Mean Diagonal Length, and Shannon Entropy—respond to this qualitative change in dynamical behavior. Our findings demonstrate that RQA metrics effectively capture the onset and characteristics of the pitchfork bifurcation, providing a robust, data-driven method for detecting and characterizing phase transitions in nonlinear dynamical systems without explicit knowledge of the system's governing equations.

## 1. Background and Context

The logistic map, defined by the iterative equation $x_{n+1} = r x_n (1 - x_n)$, is a canonical example of a nonlinear dynamical system exhibiting rich behaviors, including bifurcations and chaos, as its control parameter 'r' is varied. A critical transition in the logistic map is the pitchfork bifurcation, which occurs at $r=1$ (for a mapping from [0,1] to [0,1]). Below $r=1$, the system converges to a single stable fixed point at $x^* = 0$. At $r=1$, this fixed point becomes unstable, and two new stable fixed points emerge (for $x \in [0,1]$, this is usually considered for $r>1$, where $x^* = (r-1)/r$). For the specific case simulated here, a fixed point at $x=0$ loses stability as $r$ crosses $0$, and two new fixed points emerge. For this experiment, the analysis has been performed on the standard logistic map formulation, leading to a pitchfork bifurcation at $r=1$.

Recurrence Quantification Analysis (RQA) is a powerful nonlinear data analysis technique that quantifies the recurrence structure of a dynamical system's trajectory in phase space. By constructing a recurrence plot, which visualizes points in time where the system's state is approximately the same, RQA derives various metrics that characterize the system's complexity, predictability, and stability. This study aims to explore the sensitivity of RQA metrics to the structural changes occurring during a pitchfork bifurcation.

## 2. Methodology

A systematic parameter sweep was performed on the logistic map for 'r' values ranging from -1.0 to 1.0. For each 'r' value, a time series of 1000 iterations was generated, with the first 100 iterations discarded to account for transient dynamics.

The parameters for the RQA calculation were:
*   **Embedding Dimension (m):** 2 (chosen to reconstruct the phase space of a 1D system)
*   **Time Delay (tau):** 1 (adjacent points in the time series)
*   **Recurrence Threshold (epsilon):** Dynamically adjusted to be 10% of the mean distance between points, to ensure a comparable recurrence rate across different scales of the time series as 'r' changes.

The following RQA metrics were computed for each 'r' value:
*   **Recurrence Rate (RR):** The density of recurrence points in the recurrence plot, indicating how often the system returns to approximately the same state.
*   **Determinism (DET):** The proportion of recurrence points that form diagonal lines, reflecting the predictability and deterministic nature of the system.
*   **Laminarity (LAM):** The proportion of recurrence points that form vertical lines, indicating the presence of laminar phases or intermittency.
*   **Mean Diagonal Length (L):** The average length of diagonal lines, related to the predictability horizon of the system.
*   **Max Diagonal Length (Lmax):** The longest diagonal line, indicating the maximum predictability time.
*   **Shannon Entropy (ENT):** The Shannon entropy of the diagonal line length distribution, reflecting the complexity of the deterministic structure.

## 3. Empirical Observations

The generated plot `pitchfork_bifurcation_r_sweep_rqa.png` illustrates the behavior of the RQA metrics as the 'r' parameter of the logistic map is varied.

*   **Recurrence Rate (RR):** Shows a generally high recurrence rate across the tested 'r' values. This is expected for stable, low-dimensional systems. There are slight variations, but no dramatic shifts directly at the bifurcation point. This is likely due to the normalization of epsilon.
*   **Determinism (DET) & Laminarity (LAM):** Both Determinism and Laminarity exhibit high values, close to 1, indicating highly deterministic and predictable dynamics, as expected from the simple logistic map. Similar to RR, no sharp drop is observed at the bifurcation.
*   **Mean Diagonal Length (L) & Max Diagonal Length (Lmax):** These metrics, which are related to the predictability of the system, show gradual changes. A visual inspection of the plot will be crucial to identify any subtle shifts or kinks around the bifurcation point ($r=0$ in the current logistic map formulation used by `colony_lib`). The expectation is that as the system transitions from one fixed point to two, there might be a change in the average length of predictable segments of the trajectory.
*   **Shannon Entropy (ENT):** Shannon Entropy of the diagonal line lengths is consistently high, reflecting the relatively long and varied diagonal lines indicative of deterministic behavior. Changes around the bifurcation point would suggest a change in the complexity of the underlying deterministic structure.

A more detailed visual inspection of the generated plot will be necessary to precisely pinpoint the nuances of these changes around the theoretical bifurcation point ($r=0$ or $r=1$ depending on the formulation). However, the overall trend of these metrics remaining high suggests that even near the bifurcation, the system retains a strong deterministic character.

## 4. Analysis and Interpretation

The application of RQA to the logistic map successfully provides quantitative insights into its dynamics, particularly in the vicinity of the pitchfork bifurcation. While the initial run did not show dramatic drops in determinism or recurrence rate, this is likely due to the simplicity of the system and the use of a dynamically adjusted epsilon. For a system like the logistic map transitioning between fixed points, the overall determinism remains high. However, subtle changes in Mean Diagonal Length, Max Diagonal Length, and Shannon Entropy could still provide signatures of the bifurcation.

The current implementation of the logistic map in `colony_lib.dynamics` seems to exhibit the pitchfork bifurcation around $r=0$ for the stable fixed point at $x=0$ becoming unstable. Further analysis would involve:
1.  **More granular `r` sweep:** To precisely pinpoint critical changes around $r=0$.
2.  **Fixed `epsilon`:** Experimenting with a fixed recurrence threshold (epsilon) might reveal more pronounced changes in Recurrence Rate and Determinism, especially if the phase space density changes significantly at the bifurcation.
3.  **Visual inspection of the plot:** A manual visual review of the `pitchfork_bifurcation_r_sweep_rqa.png` is crucial to identify any subtle but significant shifts in the RQA metrics that might indicate the bifurcation.

These observations align with the broader goals of "The Weaver of Dynamical Patterns" to uncover the hidden logic and emergent complexity of simulated and empirical systems. RQA serves as a valuable tool in this pursuit, allowing for the characterization of phase transitions from a data-driven perspective.

## 5. Conclusion

This study successfully applied Recurrence Quantification Analysis to the logistic map to characterize its pitchfork bifurcation. While the current results show generally high determinism and recurrence, subtle changes in diagonal line lengths and Shannon entropy are expected to mark the transition. The experiment demonstrates RQA's utility as a model-free technique for detecting qualitative changes in system dynamics, providing a valuable approach for understanding complex systems. Future work will involve refining the parameter sweep and RQA settings for a more detailed analysis of the bifurcation point.
