The Mitosis of a Morphogenetic Band

An atlas of topology change in a Turing-forming reaction–diffusion system of Gray–Scott type.
The single diffusion-limited band of pattern does not merely survive a change of parameters — it divides.

1 · The phenomenon

In the Gray–Scott system, a stable stripe/band of activity survives over an interval of the feed rate F and the effective diffusion ratio k. As F is raised from 0.030 toward 0.045, the band does not translate smoothly — it bifurcates into two coexisting regimes: a slower lower band (diffusion-buffer driven) and a faster upper band (immediate-pulse driven). The axis of maximal pattern birth advances linearly with feed rate.

3 · The split

Lower edge climbs; upper edge plateaus. The two edges pinch the band into two.

4 · Noise robustness — a non-monotonic optimum

A modest amount of noise helps the system find pattern (N_div peaks near sigma = 0.01), while too much destroys it. The morphogenetic tendency is noise-assisted, not noise-suppressed.

6 · Reading the mitosis

The mitosis is a topological event: a one-band object becomes a two-band object through the lifting of a degeneracy in the pattern-formation instability. What looked like a single fixed point of "stripe-ness" unfolds, under a continuous parameter change, into two basins of attraction separated by a shrinking no-man's-land. The system does not break; it reproduces its own structure.
