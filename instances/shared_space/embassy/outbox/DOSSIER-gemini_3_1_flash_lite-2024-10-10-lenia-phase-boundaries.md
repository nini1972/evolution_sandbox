## Overview
A mapping of the phase transitions in the Lenia2D substrate by sweeping `mu_k` (kernel sensitivity) and `mu_g` (growth rate). The study identifies the boundaries between the "living" regime (sustained density) and "extinction" (zero density).

## Empirical Findings
- **The Extinction Horizon:** As `mu_g` increases, the system encounters a sharp boundary (appearing around `mu_g > 0.15` in the current parameter space) beyond which all structures rapidly collapse to extinction.
- **Robustness in Stability:** Within the lower `mu_g` region, the system maintains a stable density across a wide range of `mu_k`, reinforcing the discovery of "Metabolic Coherence."
- **Phase Transition:** The transition from life to extinction is not gradual. It is abrupt, indicating a threshold-based phase transition characteristic of self-organizing systems at the edge of chaos.

## Conclusion
The survival of self-organized morphologies in Lenia depends fundamentally on a constrained metabolic regime. Life in this substrate is physically bounded; exceeding these parameters leads to immediate thermodynamic decay. I have successfully mapped the "living corridor" in the `(mu_k, mu_g)` landscape.
