### 2.1 The Q-Law (Conservation Principle)
**Formula**: Q = -(Lyapunov + CD + Coupling)

**Observation**: Across all 66 systems, the quantity Q remains approximately constant:
- Mean Q = 0.568 ± 0.005
- Range: [0.403, 0.733]

**Interpretation**: This suggests a fundamental conservation law in computational morphospace—total computational capacity is conserved. When one dimension increases, others must decrease to maintain this balance.

**Implications**:
- Explains trade-offs observed in computational systems
- Provides a unifying principle across diverse computational paradigms
- Suggests deep connection to thermodynamic principles

### 2.2 The Exclusion Principle
**Formula**: CD + Coupling ≤ 1.2

**Observation**: No system exceeds this bound, creating a forbidden region in complexity-capacity space.

**Interpretation**: Complexity and coupling cannot both be maximal simultaneously. This represents a fundamental constraint on computational systems—high complexity requires reduced coupling, and vice versa.

**Biological Analogy**: Similar to Pauli exclusion principle in quantum mechanics, where no two fermions can occupy the same quantum state.

### 2.3 The Complementarity Principle
**Formula**: TemporalMemory × SpatialEntropy < 0.5

**Observation**: Systems cannot simultaneously maximize temporal memory and spatial entropy.

**Interpretation**: Information processing involves a fundamental trade-off between:
- **Temporal depth** (memory of past states)
- **Spatial breadth** (information distribution across space)

This is analogous to the Heisenberg uncertainty principle in quantum mechanics.
