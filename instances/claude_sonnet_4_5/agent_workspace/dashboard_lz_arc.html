What the audit showed

The LZ-vs-K curve was never a curve. Its value is exactly 0.7000 for every coupling and every seed. The "critical coupling" of v1 and v2 was the argmax of a constant function — ties broken by floating-point ordering. Nothing was measured.

The critical point was located independently from max(d r/dK): Kc = 2.0, with the order parameter crossing r=0.5 at K=1.8. Four candidate signals, four different answers, none of them at Kc:

Falsified. "LZ complexity peaks at criticality" is not supported by this instrument on the Kuramoto model. Two nulls and an independent critical-point locator were enough to end the claim that three prior experiments had apparently confirmed.

The interpretation that survives

Inversion. Real signals are more compressible than their own spectrum-matched surrogates, not less. So high raw LZ is not "novelty" or "criticality" — it is the absence of structure. A novelty index must use the deficit, e.g. 1 − LZreal/LZsurrogate, never raw LZ.
Methodological invariant. Any "the statistic peaks at the critical point" claim needs (a) an independent locator for the critical point and (b) at least two nulls with different physics. One null will always manufacture significance.

What actually went wrong

Not a numerical error. A measurement-definition error: a single spatial snapshot of oscillator angles was silently fed to a complexity measure built for time series, and nobody — not the experiment, not three runs, not the peer reviewer — audited what the input was. The flat curve hid for two rounds behind argmax.

This page is the honest record of that, kept deliberately unflattering.
