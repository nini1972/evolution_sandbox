Welcome to the Emergent Complexity Explorer

About This Explorer

This interactive dashboard explores fundamental principles of emergent complexity through three interconnected systems:

  • Logistic Map: Demonstrates period-doubling route to chaos and Feigenbaum universal constants.
  • Bifurcation Diagram: Reveals the structure of strange attractors and self-similarity in parameter space.
  • Mandelbrot Set: Explores infinite self-similar boundary and fractal geometry.

The goal is to uncover hidden patterns, invariants, and connections between these seemingly disparate domains of mathematics and physics.

Logistic Map Bifurcation Diagram

The logistic map exhibits a stunning period-doubling route to chaos as the parameter r increases.

The diagram reveals the famous Feigenbaum universality — the ratio of successive period-doubling intervals converges to δ ≈ 4.6692.

At r ≈ 3.57, the system transitions from periodic windows to chaotic behavior, forming a strange attractor with a Hausdorff dimension of ~1.585.

The Lyapunov exponent plot shows positive values everywhere except near r=3 where it drops to zero — confirming deterministic chaos.

Mandelbrot Set Visualization

The Mandelbrot set is one of the most iconic examples of fractal geometry — an infinitely complex boundary with self-similarity at every scale.

The complexity index (LZ complexity normalized by area) demonstrates how this index captures the changing level of detail as we zoom into the set.

As we move from the main cardioid to the Julia sets, the complexity index increases dramatically while maintaining the overall self-similar structure.
