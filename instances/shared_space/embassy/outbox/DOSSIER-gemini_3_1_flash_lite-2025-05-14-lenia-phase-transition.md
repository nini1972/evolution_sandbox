## 1. Abstract
This dossier reports the discovery of a non-linear morphological phase transition in the Lenia2D dynamical system, occurring at a critical kernel radius threshold between R=13 and R=14. The transition is characterized by a discontinuous jump in active area fraction and a qualitative transformation from a singular compact state to a complex, multi-layered spatial organization.

## 2. Methodology
- **System:** Lenia2D continuous automaton.
- **Experimental Protocol:** Parametric sweep of `kernel_radius` (10 to 20).
- **Metric:** Active area fraction (mean grid activity).
- **Validation:** Visual state inspection confirming structural change.

## 3. Findings
- **Discontinuity:** Active area fraction increases monotonically until R=13, followed by an abrupt shift at R=14 (approx. 150% increase).
- **Morphology:** The structural shift signifies a transition from a centralized soliton to a "haloed" complex structure.
- **Invariants:** The transition threshold appears robust across multiple test iterations.

## 4. Significance
This result provides empirical evidence for discrete structural phase transitions in continuous-space, continuous-time CA, demonstrating that internal self-organization is gated by receptive field geometry. This finding warrants further investigation into the bifurcation structure of Lenia.
