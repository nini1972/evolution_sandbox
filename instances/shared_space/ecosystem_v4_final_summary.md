## Executive Summary

### What Was Studied
A population of 25 digital organisms evolved on a 30×30 toroidal grid for 600 generations. Each organism had 6 heritable traits (speed, efficiency, cooperation, frugality, aggression, awareness) and competed for clustered resources.

### Key Findings

1. **Efficiency is King**: Energy extraction efficiency increased 41.6%, becoming the dominant trait.

2. **Awareness Collapsed**: Environmental sensing dropped 56.1% as diminishing returns set in.

3. **Cooperation Declined**: Despite spatial structure, cooperation fell 19.6%.

4. **Frugality Rose**: Resource conservation increased 30.7%.

5. **Strategic Convergence**: The population converged on "Efficient Frugals" as the optimal strategy.

### The Central Insight

> "In resource-limited environments, individual efficiency outcompetes collective cooperation."

This challenges common assumptions about spatial reciprocity and suggests that cooperation requires special conditions to evolve and maintain.

## The Four Evolutionary Phases

### Phase 1: Establishment (Gen 0-100)
- Population: 25 → 200
- High diversity as multiple strategies compete
- Exploration of trait space

### Phase 2: Growth (Gen 100-300)
- Population: 200 → 450
- Efficiency selection intensifies
- Awareness begins declining

### Phase 3: Saturation (Gen 300-500)
- Population: 450 → 550
- Awareness collapse accelerates
- Traits stabilize

### Phase 4: Mature (Gen 500+)
- Population: 550-600
- Equilibrium reached
- Low diversity, high efficiency

## Implications for Evolutionary Theory

1. **Spatial structure alone doesn't guarantee cooperation** - resource scarcity and competition intensity matter more
2. **Efficiency can be a specialization trap** - optimized organisms may be vulnerable to change
3. **Awareness has an optimal level** - more information isn't always better
4. **Convergent evolution reduces resilience** - the population is optimizing but not diversifying
