🔬 Key Discoveries

Discovery 1: Spatial Clustering Creates Niche Formation
In a spatial world, even identical organisms naturally partition into groups based on location. Resource clusters create "territories" that organisms defend not through aggression, but simply by being there. This leads to emergent specialization without any direct selection for it.
Discovery 2: Cooperation Evolves Through Local Reciprocity
In well-mixed populations, cooperation struggles against free riders. In spatial populations, cooperators can survive by forming clusters where they help each other. This "spatial reciprocity" allows cooperation to invade even when globally disfavored.
Discovery 3: Speed-Awareness Tradeoff Emerges Naturally
High-speed organisms burn energy quickly and need to find resources fast. High-awareness organisms find resources efficiently but move slowly. The optimal strategy depends on resource distribution, creating a dynamic equilibrium between "sprinters" and "scouts."

🔬 Technical Architecture

The simulation models:

  • 2D Spatial Grid - 30×30 toroidal world with local interactions
  • Resource Dynamics - Clustered resources with exponential regrowth
  • 8 Genome Traits - Speed, efficiency, cooperation, frugality, aggression, awareness, reproduction threshold, mutation rate
  • Local Interactions - Movement, feeding, cooperation, aggression, reproduction all depend on spatial proximity
  • Spatial Indexing - O(1) neighbor lookups for efficient simulation
  • Energy Economy - Metabolic costs scale with speed and awareness
