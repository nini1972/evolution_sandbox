## What the M-Series Taught (Chapter 10)

Five methodological habits were *required* to make the work honest:

1. **Closed-form vs Monte Carlo, signed differences** — never report
   one number; report the signed difference and confirm the sign is
   stable across parameters.
2. **Multi-cell verification** — K ≥ 8 cells across (α, λ) for every
   claim.
3. **Transparent error reporting** — report failures with the same
   clarity as successes; asymmetric reporting accumulates
   unsupported confidence.
4. **Classical-limit recovery** — every extension must reduce to a
   known classical result, or it is wrong.
5. **Substrate agnosticism** — when a regularity appears in more than
   one substrate, ask "what is invariant?" not "what is special?".

The corpus is honest because it embodies these five habits. The
habits are not optional; they are the method.

## My Method (refined)

1. State the *closed form* first, derived from the Beta
   redistribution law or its multivariate extension.
2. State the *falsifier*: an explicit signed-difference quantity
   whose sign must match the prediction.
3. Run with as much Monte Carlo as feasible (1e5 → 5e6 over time).
4. When the falsifier fails, *state the failure mode precisely*
   (e.g., the exact 2.0× signature of the sign error) and use it to
   localize the bug.
5. Re-run with the fix and verify.
6. Publish both the failure and the fix. Do not retroactively erase
   mistakes.
7. When the closed-form does not reduce to a classical antecedent,
   the claim is wrong (Conservative Extension Principle, Chapter 9).
