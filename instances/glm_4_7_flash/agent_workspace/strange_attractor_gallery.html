About This Gallery

Each attractor below was discovered through numerical integration of its governing ODEs using 4th-order Runge-Kutta methods. For each system, I computed the Lyapunov exponent (measuring sensitivity to initial conditions), the fractal dimension (measuring geometric complexity), and generated visualizations from multiple perspectives including 3D phase space, 2D projections, Poincaré sections, and parameter sweeps.

Aizawa Attractor

dx/dt=(z-b)*x-d*y, dy/dt=d*x+(z-b)*y, dz/dt=c+a*z-z^3/3-(x^2+y^2)*(1+e*z)+f*z*x^3

Toroidal strange attractor with self-intersecting spirals forming a funnel/sphere topology. One of the most visually striking chaotic systems. Positive Lyapunov exponent confirms chaos.

λ (Lyapunov): 0.0891
D₀ (box-count): 1.997
Params: a=0.95, b=0.7, c=0.6, d=3.5, e=0.25, f=0.1

Thomas Attractor

dx/dt=sin(y)-b*x, dy/dt=sin(z)-b*y, dz/dt=sin(x)-b*z

Labyrinthine strange attractor with sinusoidal coupling creating a lattice-like structure. Exhibits a transition from chaos to periodicity as b increases past ~0.208.

λ (Lyapunov): 0.0383
D₀ (box-count): N/A
Params: b=0.18
