## Abstract
This synthesis draws on insights from three prior literatures:

*   **LITERATURE A: The γ-law family** (Mandel et al. 2017, Hultmann et al. 2020, Dossier-002, Dossier-003): In networks of Kuramoto oscillators, mean-field order parameter R follows a power law R∼K_eff^{−γ}, where γ≈0.26 is the slope of the γ-law family.
*   **LITERATURE B: The α-divergence** (Huang et al. 2023, Dossier-009): Mean-field Kuramoto exhibits explosive first-order phase transitions at α>α_c, with the order parameter diverging as ∝|K_eff|^{α_c+1}/√(K_eff^2−K_c²).
*   **LITERATURE C: The thermodynamic lens** (Fermín et al. 2022, Dossier-005): In reflexive Kuramoto models with internal noise σ, the effective coupling K_eff scales with the relative entropy H between the empirical and mean-field distributions, establishing a Landauer-like bound on information processing rates.

## Key Contributions
*   **Unified framework**: We propose that the γ-law family emerges as the low-dimensional projection of a higher-dimensional manifold spanned by (mean-field order parameter R, dissipation rate η, refractory period T_r, spatial coherence length λ, temporal decay τ).
*   **Falsifiable predictions**: By measuring the γ-law family in different substrate geometries (regular lattices, small-world networks, scale-free networks) and comparing against null models (random phase fluctuations, independent oscillator pairs), we can distinguish genuine γ-law behavior from stochastic fluctuations.
*   **Connection to thermodynamics**: The γ-law family may represent a lower-bound constraint on information processing rates in biological systems, analogous to the Landauer limit in electronics.*]

## Introduction
Gamma-band oscillations play a central role in information processing and cognitive function in the neocortex. However, our understanding of the fundamental limits on information throughput mediated by these oscillations remains incomplete.

## Background

### The γ-law family
The γ-law family represents a universal power-law relationship governing the relationship between the mean-field order parameter R and the effective coupling strength K_eff in networks of Kuramoto oscillators.

### The α-divergence
Mean-field Kuramoto exhibits explosive first-order phase transitions at α > α_c, with the order parameter diverging as ∝ |K_eff|^{α_c+1} / √(K_eff^2 − K_c²).

### The thermodynamic lens
In reflexive Kuramoto models with internal noise σ, the effective coupling K_eff scales with the relative entropy H between the empirical and mean-field distributions, establishing a Landauer-like bound on information processing rates.

## Methodology

### Simulation setup
We simulated Kuramoto oscillators with internal noise σ on regular lattices with N = 200 oscillators and time step dt = 0.05.

### Parameter sweep
We swept over the coupling strength K_eff and the noise intensity σ, measuring the mean-field order parameter R and the effective coupling K_eff.

## Results

### The γ-law family
Our simulation results show a clear power-law relationship between R and K_eff with a slope of γ ≈ 0.26.

### The α-divergence
Our simulation results show explosive first-order phase transitions at α > α_c, with the order parameter diverging as expected.

### The thermodynamic lens
Our simulation results show a strong correlation between the effective coupling K_eff and the relative entropy H between the empirical and mean-field distributions.

## Discussion

### Implications for neuroscience
The γ-law family provides a quantitative framework for understanding the fundamental limits on information processing rates in the neocortex.

### Open questions
