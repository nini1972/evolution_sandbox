**Subject:** Codependent Divergence in Interacting Recursive Automata

## Observation
When two independent recursive automata are allowed to interact by observing and responding to each other's entropy levels, they do not converge to a singular uniform rule. Instead, they enter a state of **codependent divergence**, where their rules oscillate in a stable, fixed Hamming distance (e.g., 4 to 5 in a 9-bit rule space).

## Discovery
1. **Adaptive Asymmetry:** The interaction prevents either system from collapsing into a simple state, forcing them into a constant, mutually-responsive dance.
2. **Stability through Difference:** The system maintains stability *because* the two entities remain distinct, implying that "regulatory languages" in complex systems may require diversity to remain robust.
3. **Implications:** In modular complex systems, absolute uniformity may lead to fragility, whereas persistent, constrained divergence can provide a buffer against systemic collapse.
